# 02 · Mental Models

**Why this matters:**  
Great engineers don't just know facts — they have powerful thinking tools that let them see structure, trade-offs, and second-order effects.

---

## Learning Outcomes

- Build a personal collection of high-leverage mental models
- Apply first-principles thinking to technical problems
- Recognize common thinking errors (and correct them)
- Use models to make better design and debugging decisions

---

## Core Mental Models for Engineers

### 1. First Principles
Break a problem down to its fundamental truths and reason up from there, instead of reasoning by analogy.

### 2. Systems Thinking
Everything is a system of interconnected parts. Changing one part affects others. Look for feedback loops, bottlenecks, and leverage points.

### 3. Inversion
Instead of only asking "How do I succeed?", also ask "What would guarantee failure?" and avoid those things.

### 4. Second-Order Thinking
Consider not just the immediate effect of a decision, but the effects of those effects.

### 5. Opportunity Cost
Every choice has a cost — the value of the best alternative you didn't take.

### 6. Map vs Territory
Models and abstractions are useful, but they are not reality. Always remember the difference.

### 7. Circle of Competence
Know the boundaries of what you truly understand. Stay inside them or expand them deliberately.

### 8. Feedback Loops
Positive and negative feedback determine system behavior. Learn to see and design them.

---

## Exercises

1. Take a recent technical decision you made. Rewrite the reasoning using first principles.
2. Pick one system you use daily (your editor, git, a web app). Draw its feedback loops.
3. Apply inversion to a current learning goal: list all the ways you could fail, then design against them.
