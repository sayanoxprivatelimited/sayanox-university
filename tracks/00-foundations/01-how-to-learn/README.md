# 01 · How to Learn

**Why this matters:**  
Most people waste years studying inefficiently. The quality of your learning system determines how far you can go.

---

## Learning Outcomes

- Understand the difference between passive and active learning
- Apply retrieval practice, spaced repetition, and interleaving
- Diagnose your own weak learning habits
- Design a personal study protocol

---

## Core Ideas

### 1. Active > Passive
Reading, watching, and highlighting feel productive but produce weak memory.  
**Doing** (solving, explaining, building, teaching) produces strong memory.

### 2. Retrieval Practice
The act of trying to recall information strengthens memory far more than re-reading.

### 3. Spaced Repetition
Reviewing material just before you would forget it is dramatically more efficient than massed practice.

### 4. Desirable Difficulty
Learning that feels easy usually is. Struggle (within reason) is a feature, not a bug.

### 5. Generation Effect
You remember better what you generate yourself than what you passively consume.

---

## Practical Protocol (Use This)

For every new technical topic:

1. **Preview** (5–10 min) — skim structure, identify key questions
2. **First pass** — read / watch actively, take minimal notes
3. **Retrieval** — close the material and write everything you remember
4. **Gap analysis** — go back only for what you missed
5. **Application** — solve problems or build something small
6. **Explain** — write a short explanation in your own words
7. **Schedule review** — 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 1 month later

---

## Exercises

1. Pick one topic you studied in the last month. Without looking at notes, write everything you still remember. Then compare. Note the gaps.
2. Design your personal study protocol for the next 30 days (time blocks, review system, tools).
3. Teach a technical concept you know to an imaginary beginner in writing (max 400 words).
